Milky Way - A Musical Galaxy for Toddlers

Milky Way is a browser-based musical galaxy for toddlers with minimal language. Every prototype below shares the same planets, the same always-consonant scale and the same soft, warm sound, so nothing can sound wrong and no text is needed. I explored two areas of the design: mapping and feedback. Each area has three prototypes, and each prototype is a separate branch published with GitHub Pages.

Area 1: Mapping

My first area of exploration is mapping: how a child's gesture becomes sound. A toddler cannot read labels or follow steps, so the gesture itself has to be the whole instruction. The three prototypes give three different answers. In the first prototype the child makes each sound by choosing it, in the second sound follows the child's movement, and in the third the galaxy plays itself and the child reshapes it. This is also where my extended technique sits. The orbiting prototype needs precisely timed notes, so it schedules them on the Web Audio clock instead of triggering sound from timers or events. I am interested in how much control a young child can have over timing and pitch without ever being asked to understand either.

Prototype 1 - Tap a Planet

This prototype is the most direct mapping. Tapping or clicking a planet plays that planet's note. Each planet has a hit area larger than it looks, so imprecise taps still land. It is a baseline to compare the other two against, and it follows the one-action simplicity of Holdspace, where a single gesture is the entire interface.

Prototype 2 - Hold and Drag

This prototype is inspired by Plink where holding the mouse down plays a note and moving changes it smoothly. Here all the planets are on screen, and while the child holds and drags, the sound glides between the planets' notes. Lifting the finger stops it. It carries over Plink's continuous, sliding response, which suits the loose, exploratory way a toddler moves.

Prototype 3 - Orbiting Planets

This prototype borrows from the orrery, the mechanical models where planets rotate around a sun. The planets orbit a central star at different speeds and each plays its note as it passes a fixed point, so the music loops on its own. The child can drag a planet nearer or further to change its speed. It treats the loop as the instrument, with the child shaping music that is already playing.

Area 2: Feedback

My second area of exploration is feedback: how the screen shows that sound is happening. My design analysis ruled out labels and text, as in Diamond Synth, so the visuals have to tell a child that they made something happen. These three prototypes keep one fixed sound behaviour and change only the visual response, from a still glow to motion to a moving world.

Prototype 4 - Glow

This prototype is the simplest visual response. A planet brightens and its halo grows while its note sounds, then fades as the sound fades. It comes straight from the glowing planets in my Assignment 1 concept, and uses one clear change, brightness, for the child to notice.

Prototype 5 - Ripples and Pulses

This prototype shows sound as motion. Each note sends rings expanding outward from its planet, like a drop in water. It takes cues from the sound waves drawn around the cursor in Plink, moving the feedback from brightness to movement, which young children tend to follow more easily.
