Milky Way - A Musical Galaxy for Toddlers

Milky Way is a browser-based musical galaxy for toddlers with minimal language. Every prototype below shares the same planets, the same always-consonant scale and the same soft, warm sound, so nothing can sound wrong and no text is needed. I explored two areas of the design: mapping and feedback. Each area has three prototypes, and each prototype is a separate branch published with GitHub Pages.

Area 1: Feedback

This area is about how the instrument shows a child that they made the sound, with no text or instruction to help. For children with minimal language, the visuals have to carry that link between action and result, and they need to stay gentle enough not to startle anyone. The three prototypes change how much the screen responds: nothing beyond the sound, a glow on the planet, or rings spreading outward. I want to find out how much visual response a toddler needs to feel in control, and whether a quieter or livelier response suits curiosity-led play better.

Prototype 1 - Tap a Planet

Tapping a planet plays its note and the screen otherwise stays the same. It uses a single gesture, as Holdspace does, and it gives me a plain reference point for judging what the other two add. The planets are scattered loosely across the screen in different sizes and colours, so a child can tell them apart without any labels.

Prototype 2 - Glow

A tapped planet glows softly for as long as its note sounds, so the light and the sound happen together. I picked a glow because it stays on the planet and moves very little, which fits the warm, soft sound and shouldn't startle a small child. It asks whether a small, contained response is enough to connect touch with sound.

Prototype 3 - Ripples

Tapping a planet sends rings outward from it, a bit like sound travelling through space. Where the glow stays on the planet, the rings reach into the space around it, so the whole screen seems to react, which suits the galaxy theme. The rings fade as they widen, so they are easy to spot across the screen without being harsh.

Area 2: Mapping and Looping

This area is about how a child's touch or movement turns into sound, and whether the instrument can build layers of music with little effort from the player. It also holds my extended technique, looping. Mapping decides how much control the child has. Looping hands some of the musical work to the instrument, so a handful of taps can sound composed. All three prototypes draw on the same consonant scale, so no mapping can produce a 'wrong' note. They differ in how directly the child shapes pitch and how much repeats without them.

Prototype 4 - Plink

This prototype is inspired by Plink where the planets are lined up down the left side with horizontal lanes running across the screen. The child presses a planet and drags up or down to change its note, so vertical movement maps straight onto pitch and the sound follows the hand. That smooth response suits a toddler's loose, exploratory movement. The lanes are tied to notes in the scale, so the wider range of control still can't land on an unpleasant sound.

Prototype 5 - Orbit Loop

The planets sit on elliptical orbits around a central Sun. Tapping the Sun starts things off, and each planet tapped afterwards joins in and keeps looping. Active planets get a bright outline and inactive ones stay dim. Where a planet sits in its orbit hints at rhythm, so repetition comes out of the child's own choices, with no step sequencer involved. That keeps looping passive and forgiving, in line with my A1 decision to leave out complex sequencing tools.

Prototype 6 - Ripples Loop

This joins the loose galaxy layout and ripples from Prototype 5 to the Sun-then-planets looping of Prototype 3. Tapping the Sun starts the loop, and each planet tapped after that pulses with glowing rings in time with its note, so the layered music can be seen as well as heard. I want to see whether looping still reads clearly without orbit lines, and whether ripples can show repetition as well as single taps. The Sun's glow shows that the system is running.
